# Hooks — the first 3 seconds decide everything

Where these rules come from: they were earned one reaction at a time while loya (the founder of LYRC, a lyric-video app for musicians) built LYRC's motion ads. His verbatim notes are kept below, marked "from the LYRC build", because they show the taste and the stakes. The owner's own notes get added the same way over time. "The owner" = whoever runs this business and gives the final yes. "The viewer" = the owner's customer.

The bar, verbatim (from the LYRC build):
- *"hooks that will stop the scroll… from a 'shit i need to watch the rest' standpoint. the first sentence matters so much. cant feel like an ad. has to feel like hey im about to get so much sauce that i have to watch this because it will benefit me directly."*
- *"we reallllyyyyy wanna prioritize like engaging/edgy/unexpected hooks so that the people will actually watch. but besides the actual text and/or voice, should have a sweet ass visual hook too. all diff animations for text, cool graphics maybe, maybe real footage, lots of motion but also enough time for the audience to actually read or understand whats going on and what the video premise is… a lot of effort needs to be spent on the hook."*

A hook is **words × visual**. Each half should stop a thumb on its own: the words with the sound off, the visual before the words land. If installed, REQUIRED: use the `viral-hooks` skill for craft and run the `anti-ai-writing` skill on every line. This file adds the motion-ad system.

## The words: value + open loop FIRST, then edgy
**The formula** (from the LYRC build, killing "didn't you post this yesterday?"): *"hooks need to tell u exactly what u rgonna get in terms of value but not reveal the entire sauce. also curiosity keeps people watching. has to be good enough to get them to watch more but not so good that they already know the answer… confusion = scroll."* Same core as the `viral-hooks` skill: topic clarity + on-target curiosity.
- **Sell the feeling, not the tool** (from the LYRC build: *"they dont want to make more videos, they want their art to be appreciated and they want to feel acceptance from others, from their peers"*). The viewer doesn't want the product or the output; they want the RESULT it buys them, as a feeling: to be seen, praised, respected, relieved, caught up, chosen. For LYRC's artists that was strangers asking "who is this?", streams from people they don't know, the family that called it a phase going quiet. The value in a hook is that result, never "more videos / posts / content" (the tool, the output, the activity); the body shows how. The pain side is emotional too: the song they poured everything into got 40 streams, a worse song blew up. **Count in the unit THEY measure**: streams for an artist; bookings, sales, hours back, compliments, full chairs, covers on a Tuesday for you. Not likes or views, unless likes or views are literally what the viewer is counting.
  - Loved on the LYRC build (for artists): *"If you want strangers in your comments asking 'who is this?', steal this."* · *"If you've watched a worse song than yours blow up, here's what they did that you didn't."*
  - Parallels for other businesses (shapes, write your own): *"If you want strangers asking 'where did you get that?', steal this."* (clothing brand) · *"If you've watched a worse barbershop fill its Saturday, here's what they did that you didn't."*
- **Say the value.** In ~1 s the viewer knows what they'll GET by watching, as a feeling or a result they want (see above), not a feature count.
- **Hold back the how.** The method, the tool, the trick, the reveal stay in the body. If the hook alone answers the question, nobody needs the video.
- **Clear beats clever.** A line that needs context ("didn't you post this yesterday?", "45,000+ names taken") is confusing, and confusion = scroll. Edgy and unexpected are seasoning on top of a clear promise, never a substitute.
- **Test:** a stranger can finish "watching this will get me ___", AND can't yet say how.
- **The ABC check** (from @seangstewart's hook reel, which the LYRC owner sent to write hooks from). Every line needs all three:
  - **A, Audience:** name a specific viewer in their own words, usually as an "if" ("if your streams die two days after release").
  - **B, Benefit:** imply what watching gets them, and make it the emotional result (praise, respect, being heard), not the means.
  - **C, Curiosity:** leave the gap open ("what are the mistakes?", which only the video answers).
  His own example: *"If nobody's watching your yaps, you're probably making the same mistakes I make."* The gold standard below passes too: A = your next song rollout, B = steal this, C = what "this" is.
- **The gold standard (the LYRC owner's own line):** *"if you were smart you would steal this for your next song rollout."*
  - **Identity friction:** it pokes their self-image ("wait, I AM smart"), so they watch to prove it.
  - **"steal this":** a tutorial framed as insider sauce, never "my product".
  - **"your next song rollout":** the audience and topic are clear in 1 s.
  - Why it beat 6 value-promise lines: *"its still a tutorial but its not like 'hey this is my boring tutorial to get you to buy my product'."* Reach for this shape first: challenge who they think they are, then promise the thing that proves it. If they can't fill the blank, it's vague. If they can already say how, it gave the sauce away.
  - Parallel shape for a generic local business (write your own): *"if you were smart you would steal this for your next slow Tuesday."*

0. **The stranger test (first).** Someone who has never heard of the brand, thumb already moving, must get in ~1 s what this is about and why it's THEIR problem.
   - Anything that needs the brand's context fails: a feature name, an internal term (what a "name" or "slug" is), the brand's own count. The LYRC owner on "45,000+ names already taken": *"ppl dont care they dont even know what it is for theyre scrolling asap."*
   - A number only works in a hook if it counts something the viewer already cares about: their streams, posts, money, time, fans, customers.
   - The brand's own counts are proof for the body, not hooks.
1. **The ME test.** Finish: *"this changes what you do with ___ tonight."* If the blank won't fill with something the viewer owns (their song, their release, their posting; their menu, their next launch, their Saturday), it's trivia. The LYRC owner rejected true, well-formed hooks for failing this.
2. **Research first, template second.** Get the specific thing first: a real number, a platform rule, a data point from the business, a mechanic of the viewer's situation. THEN pick the angle. Sources:
   - `BRAND.md`: the proof sources, and the claims with where each is verified (verified numbers for the BODY's proof; in a hook only if they pass the stranger test);
   - the owner's real numbers (their sales, bookings, results, usage);
   - the customer's real situations: what they google, what they text, what they complain about, what they almost did instead;
   - reels research (e.g. IG treats identical reposts as copies; a new edit counts as original);
   - the `viral-hooks` and `anti-ai-writing` skills, if installed;
   - the product or service itself.
3. **Edgy = a double-take.** A line they didn't expect an ad to say. Tools:
   - say the quiet part out loud (what your customers think but don't say or post);
   - a provocation aimed at a situation: the designer who ghosts, the "free" thing that charges, the lazy version everyone posts;
   - a blunt real number;
   - flipping advice everyone repeats.
   Test: would they send it to a friend, or argue with it in the comments? A line that sounds like an ad opener ("introducing", "meet", the brand name first) fails.
4. **Edgy stays true and aims at situations, not people.** No invented stories ("I stopped posting for 11 days"), no fake stats, no fake urgency. Never insult the viewer, a named person or anyone's identity. A first-person line ("i", "my") is the owner speaking: use it only if it's literally their true story AND they OKed that line. Flag it and wait for their yes, even when the check-in is skipped.
5. **Second person, and line 1 carries a number or a named thing** when one honestly exists. Short: 4–14 words total, 1–4 lines.
6. **Never tear down what the brand sells.** Aim at the lazy version (the black-screen post, the same clip on every page), so the brand becomes more necessary. The "bad" thing in a hook is never the brand's own product or output: a decaying or boring example of what the brand makes makes the brand look broken (caught by the critic on an early LYRC build, where the "bad" video was a LYRC lyric video).
7. **The body must pay it off.** Whatever the hook promises, the body shows it happening for real. LYRC examples: "up to 30 videos. one click."; "3 tries. under 10 minutes."

## The visual hook: the most engaging part of the video
From the LYRC build: *"hooks need to be the most engaging part of the video to get ppl to watch the rest of the video, otherwise they will scroll and the rest of the video doesn't matter."*
- **A story hook is fast, but clean.** Fewer, slower moves that each hold long enough to read, and clear layers (words, then characters, then background). Background crowds and textures stay calm and dark, well away from the type. From the LYRC build, on a stadium-crowd shot: *"could just be cleaner. doesnt need to be that fast cutting. blow up idea is good but it just looks super fuckin jumbled rn."*
- **Letter-heads are heads.** When letters stand in for characters' heads, each letter sits ON the body where the head goes, with its bottom at the top of the neck, never sliding down over the neck or shoulders. Check every shot it's in. From the LYRC build: *"make sure the letters on WORSE are a little higher so theyre actually like the heads not overlapping their necks lmao."*

**The bar: the story hooks** in `examples/story-hooks/{A-stick-figures,B-footage-crowd,C-words-act-it-out}/` (from the LYRC build: *"wtf these are insanely better… that shit is fire"*). Three takes on one line, each acting out its story; C ("the words act it out") was the pick. They were made for LYRC: learn HOW from them, never copy their subject, track, layouts or hook. Read the README, watch all three, then write a NEW story for your line:
- **Act out the story the line tells.** The picture shows the situation the line describes, with characters and action and a twist. In the LYRC examples: the artist posting into silence, strangers stampeding in, comments piling onto him, a hand stealing the thing. A fast animated mini-story, edgy and funny, never an abstract world with three words on it. (From the LYRC build, the three-words-on-a-3D-wall hooks before it were *"pretty ass visually"*; the same world on every hook made them all read alike.)
- **Every spoken word can ride along as kinetic type that does what it says**: in the LYRC examples, letter-people spell STRANGERS, COMMENTS piles up, WHO IS THIS? gets spray-painted, a glove snatches STEAL THIS. That's acting, not captions. Captions are words sitting in a strip; these words are characters in the scene.
- **Pick a style that fits the line**: stick figures / line animation, the owner's real footage + graphic characters, or the words themselves as the cast. Drawn in code, physical (squash, anticipation, a camera with weight), never clip-art.
- **Fast is right in a hook, as long as every word reads.** The loved story hooks measure 0–9 % calm (`pace_check --hook` is advisory). Each printed word still holds long enough to read, and the premise lands by ~2.5 s. The calm rules are for the BODY (the LYRC owner's note on a body that failed them: *"way too fast and too much going on"*), never the hook.
- **The track still does the work**: its dropout or slam lands on the twist (the reveal, the punchline).
- **Made, not generated.** It must pass `STYLE.md` → Craft: no web card, no boxes or chips around words, physics moves, layers, texture and light. From the LYRC build, on a redacted-sheet hook: *"the hook especially looks super vibecoded. and not enough in animations."*
- **Craft references, not formats:** `examples/the-lift/` ("fucking SICK" at the time, on the LYRC build: a 3D wall of real product renders, the track's dropout doing the stealing, a camera with weight) still shows camera and music-sync craft. Don't reuse its world as a hook template.
- **Not static-literal.** A still illustration of the line is boring; an animated story with a twist is the opposite. From the LYRC build, on a hook that showed "same video every day" as a grid of identical reposts: *"hook is awful rn cuz its so boring and no text and its literally the thing we tryna get away from which is boring predictable visuals."* Find the image that adds a second layer: surprise, scale, a metaphor, a pattern broken.
- **The hook always has designed type**, narrated or not: at least its 2–3 key words, and often every spoken word, acting inside the scene (see the story hooks).
- **Frame 0 is the cover.** At t=0 the frame is already full, striking and moving, good enough to be the reel's thumbnail:
  - full-bleed footage or the product mid-action;
  - big type or a big graphic already on screen;
  - a slam or whip already under way.
  Never black with something entering, and never a blurred thing in a corner. (Both early LYRC test builds opened nearly empty.)
- **Fill the frame.** The hook owns the whole 9:16.
  - The focal element is BIG: a headline at 130–200 px, or footage / the real screen full-bleed or ≥80% of the width. Scale the camera up; don't leave a small phone on black.
  - No region bigger than ~⅓ of the frame sits empty or as flat texture for more than ~0.5 s. Layers fill it: footage behind, the graphic in the middle, words in front.
  - In the early LYRC test builds the text crowded the top third over dead tiles, and a small phone sat on black. Both read as small and empty.
- **No dead frames.** A full-screen flood never holds a flat colour for more than ~0.15 s; the next scene is already revealing as it completes. An entrance never passes through other text.
- **The first frame after the hook lands big too.** Zoom the camera into the real product screen so it fills the frame; tiny UI on black is the same failure.
- **The screen changes every 1–2 s, from frame 0** (from the LYRC build, comparing an ad to Shopify Editions: *"notice how its moving from the start, and its not staying on the same screen for more than a second or two. u didnt change the first screen until 4 seconds in… this is literally the most important part"*). Plan 3–4 screens before `HOOK_END` (Shopify: logo 1 s → wall + "150+" 1 s → "TO GROW YOUR BUSINESS" 1.5 s → feature screen at 3.75 s). The phrase can build across screens, one chunk per screen. Shopify's hook measures 13% calm, so for hooks the calm bar is a floor of ~25%, not 45%; the reading rule still holds.
- **Footage behind a fast hook**: slow it (½ speed; ⅓–¼ with `minterpolate=mi_mode=mci`, run at tile size so it's quick), denoise night footage (`hqdn3d`), and dim it while type lands, so the words win.
- **Every text element gets its own move.** Take moves from `STYLE.md` (whip, per-letter drop + bounce, pop, flip, number slam, accent write-on) and new ones: typewriter with a caret, mask wipe, a word that splits or shatters, a word swapped for another, a strike-through, a scale-through-camera.
  - Let words act out what they say: "dropping" drops, "ghosted" greys out, a price tag rips.
  - Never give every line the same line-rise. That was the look of the first posted LYRC reels, and those reels got skipped.
- **Layers in motion.** Foreground words, a midground graphic, and background footage or a living texture, moving at different speeds (parallax, a slow camera drift; never pulses on the beat). Lots of motion, living in the layers, the camera and the letters. Words hold their position while being read, but stay alive (the layers and camera drift around them); they don't freeze.
- **Real footage or real product beats illustration.**
  - the owner's real clips (footage they shot, picked by file name);
  - the real product or screen mid-action, rebuilt;
  - their real photos, packaging, work and results;
  - the track's own waveform.
  Real reads as "this is real". Graphics (stickers, halftone, rebuilt IG chrome, receipts, game screens) carry the concept. Never fake UI, never AI-generated footage passed off as the business, never stock that pretends to be them.
- **Readable, or it didn't happen.**
  - One focal point at a time.
  - Each line stays fully readable for at least 0.25 s per word + 0.4 s before anything else competes for the eye. A small label (≤3 words, mono) that arrives after the line is readable doesn't count as competing.
  - Real UI text is small (a comment, a search bar). Zoom the camera until it's ≥110 px on screen.
  - High contrast, ≥110 px.
  - The premise (what this video is about and why they should care) lands by ~2.5 s.
  - The budget is tight: two lines rarely fit the reading rule before a 3–4 s `HOOK_END`. Keep line 1 ≤4 words (a big number can BE line 1), or carry line 2 into scene 1 as its headline.
- **Length ~2.5–4 s, ending on a measured event.** `HOOK_END` is a BASS IN from `music_prep.py map`. If the hook needs more reading time, pick a track window whose first bass entry comes later. Don't speed up the words.
- **Sound is half the visual.** A hit on each move, the narrator line if there is one, and the track's intro building under it.

## Promos for a single product, drop or event (the owner's own release)
On the LYRC build this was loya's own song release. The same rules fit a menu item, a collection drop, a grand opening, an event. The job is to make them want the thing itself (press play, come in, grab the drop), not to sell the brand. So:
- The ME test becomes "a feeling the viewer already owns": the 2 am text, the ex, the drive; for a drop or opening, the same kind of moment in their own life.
- The payoff is the thing itself landing (for a song, its drop).
- A number is optional.
- The CTA is the action the thing itself needs (listen, shop the drop, book the night), not a comment keyword.
- Still: frame 1 moving, every element its own move, readable, the premise by ~2.5 s.

## Opening devices (each of the 6 ideas uses a different one)
From the LYRC build: *"whatever we do we def need to focus the most on the hook, both visually and verbally."* The first posted LYRC reels all opened on the brand mascot whispering and got skipped a lot. A hook built on a DM thread going unanswered ("still waiting on your cover art?") was *"fucking money"*.
- **A real-life thread**: DMs / texts / notifications the viewer recognises (the unanswered designer DM).
- **Result first**: the finished thing slams in before a word is said.
- **A narrator line**: a real voice (ElevenLabs via `scripts/voice.py`, `PIPELINE.md` §4, `NARRATION.md`) says the hook; the words land synced to it.
- **A clock / counter**: a real time or number from the build running up.
- **POV card**: a calendar, a lock screen, a notification — the moment they've lived.
- **A brand mascot or character**, if the brand has one: one option among many, never the default. Rest it for a few ads.
- **Cold open on the product**: the product mid-action (an app's prompt typing, a setting snapping on, the pan mid-flip, the clippers on the first pass) with the line on top.
- **Reel comments**: the comment sheet slides up on a real-looking reel ("where'd you get that??"), generic handles, clearly illustrative.
- **Search bar**: a query types itself (what they'd google at 2 am about their problem).
- **Split screen**: the lazy version vs the brand's version, judged in one look.
- **Slot machine / flip board**: options rolling and landing (which link, which look, which day).
- **Crossed-out list**: a to-do or budget with items struck through one by one.
- **Tile wall**: dozens of real outputs filling the frame, then one pulls forward. The loved "lift" (`examples/the-lift/`) is this device plus a heist, synced to the track's dropout.
- **Game / title screen**: a start screen, a lives or score counter, a "level 1" card that fits the track's or the brand's world.
- **A dare**: a direct challenge ("pause on any frame…") that the body then pays off.
- **Receipt / invoice**: the real cost of the old way, itemized.
- **Scratch card**: a coin scratches off a "try again" (one shot vs many).
- **Leaked / redacted document**: a play with its steps blacked out; the end card un-redacts it ("now it's yours."). Built on the LYRC build for the "steal this" line; pairs with identity-friction lines. It was first built as a clean white card with highlighter boxes, a DO NOT SHARE outline and a sticker, and the LYRC owner called it *"super vibecoded"*. If used again: a real sheet of paper (texture, a fold, marker redaction with ink bleed, a real rubber-stamp impression) in a real place (the owner's own space, on camera), a moving camera with depth of field; or drop the paper and let the hero word itself be the thing that gets stolen.

Rules:
- UI devices (comments, a search bar, DMs, a post) are the real app or IG rebuilt 1:1; every other device is built as a real object or a motion-design world, never a flat HTML card (`STYLE.md` → Craft).
- If the brand has a mascot, at most 1 idea opens on it.
- At least 3 devices not in the previous ad's "Hook devices" cell (`ads-log.md`).
- Add new devices here when the owner likes one.

## 6 ideas, build the best one
When the owner has already picked the hook, build it and list 5 alternatives briefly for the record; don't re-litigate the pick.
1. **Research** the subject (rule 2). Write 10+ candidate lines across ≥5 angles.
2. **Audit** every line: the ME test, anti-ai-writing, a fact-check, the double-take test. Cut hollow contrasts and anything unverifiable.
3. **Six ideas.** Each is a line plus a device plus a visual concept: what's on frame 1, how each element moves, which footage or graphic, the sound. They need different angles AND different devices.
4. **Rank them honestly**, scoring 1–5 on each:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **clear value** | can a stranger finish "watching this gets me ___" after ~1 s? (GATE: under 4 = cut the idea, however clever) |
| **open loop** | is the how withheld, so they have to watch to get it? (GATE: under 4 = cut; if the hook already answers it, it gave the sauce away) |
| stranger | does someone who's never heard of the brand get why it's their problem in ~1 s? (a 1–2 here = cut the idea) |
| stops the thumb | would it stop you in 0.5 s, sound off? |
| premise | is it clear by 2.5 s? |
| their stake | does it pass the ME test? |
| double-take | is it edgy or unexpected? |
| visual surprise | would a viewer have predicted this picture from the line? Literal illustration = 1: "same video" shown as a grid of identical posts is the *"boring predictable visuals"* the LYRC owner wanted gone. Aim for a picture that makes the line land harder: a metaphor, a pattern interrupt, a scale jump, the real product doing something unexpected |
| payoff | does the body deliver it? |
| real | can it be built with real assets? |

   The top total is the pick; break ties toward the edgier one.
5. **Storyboard the pick every 0.25 s**, from 0 to `HOOK_END`: what moves, each element's move by name, what the viewer can read at that moment, the sound.
6. **Build it first.** It's a `hook` value in `scripts/starter.html`. Render with the body still empty, then check the 0.25 s frame sheet and the IG overlay. Iterate until it's the best thing in the ad; only then build the body.
7. **Deliver the ad with all 6 ideas and their scores.**
   - If the owner picks another idea, build it as a second `hook` value.
   - On the LYRC build every ad also went out as trial reels (Instagram's test posts to non-followers), no review needed: after the pick ships, build #2 and #3 as trial-reel hooks on the same body. Their results decide which hook gets paid money. Whether trial reels skip the owner's review here is the owner's call.
   - If the owner asks for hook versions or tests, build the top 2–6 (each with its own `HOOKV` SFX block, all hidden under the transition at `HOOK_END`) and name them `<Brand> <name> ad N - <angle>.mp4`.
   - ads-log's "Hook devices" cell lists only hooks that were BUILT.
8. **Log results.** When the owner reports numbers, add them to `hooks-log.md` and seed the next round from the winner: same angle, new wording, new visual.

## Posted lines (never reuse the words; the angles are fair game)
Log every line you post in `hooks-log.md` (the words, the date, the device, the results) and never reuse the words of a posted line; the angle is fair game for a new wording and a new visual. The first six hooks posted on the LYRC build covered these angles: an identity question, a POV stake, a callout of the lazy version, the math, a how-to, an honest catch. All six were lines rising over living backgrounds, and they got skipped a lot, which is why the visual hook now gets equal weight.

Untested shapes (shapes only; write the words for your subject):
- **the correction**: common advice, then the precise version of it.
- **the admission**: a true, slightly embarrassing industry fact the viewer suspected.
- **the autopsy**: one real case's verified result, taken apart (a customer's only with their permission).
- **the ban list**: N things that hurt their result (a real count).

## Copy mechanics
- The payoff phrase goes in Instrument Serif italic in the ad's accent colour; everything else is Space Grotesk 600. (That is the default house kit; if `brand.json` names the brand's own fonts, they replace it with the same roles.)
- Lines fit ≤920 px wide at 112–168 px. Split a line rather than shrink below ~110 px.
- A number that "slams" stays hidden until its moment and animates once. A visible number that re-slams reads as a glitch.
